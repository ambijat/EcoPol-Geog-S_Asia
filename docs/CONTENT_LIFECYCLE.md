# Classroom-First Content Lifecycle

Production is organised backwards from the classroom-ready weekly lecture pair. The historical-source census and repository-wide audit support production but must not obstruct preparation of the next scheduled Tuesday and Friday classes.

```text
Historical lecture deck
+ instructor material
+ approved offline sources
+ verified online sources
        ↓
lecture-level source audit
        ↓
weekly intellectual architecture
        ↓
Tuesday Part A design
        ↓
Friday Part B design
        ↓
instructor review
        ↓
Tuesday editable deck and PDF
        ↓
Friday editable deck and PDF
        ↓
actual classroom delivery
        ↓
separate post-class records
        ↓
instructor validation
        ↓
academically consequential ledger record
        ↓
revision for the next course cycle
```

## Gates

1. **Registration:** establishes identity and provenance, not truth or permission.
2. **Sanitisation:** removes or isolates technical and presentational risks without concealing changes.
3. **Verification:** checks substance independently of sanitisation.
4. **Pedagogical adaptation:** converts verified material for a stated learning purpose while separating instructor content, external evidence, AI synthesis, and provisional interpretation.
5. **Instructor decision:** only the instructor may approve, reject, publish, or authorise final classroom use.
6. **Retrospective record:** documents what was actually used and taught rather than assuming the plan occurred.

## Just-in-time production

```text
approve semester-level lecture map
→ develop the next Tuesday and Friday pair
→ instructor review
→ teach
→ record actual conduct
→ continue to the next pair
```

Repository-wide auditing may proceed in parallel. It does not create a prerequisite that the entire archive be classified before the next lecture pair can be prepared.

## Weekly deliverable gate

Each part must meet `docs/CLASSROOM_DELIVERABLE_STANDARD.md`. The Part A and Part B decks are reviewed as one coherent weekly argument, while each retains its own teaching brief, provenance map, verification record, editable deck, PDF, and post-class record.

Routine formatting, file movement within authorised project areas, test runs, and technical refactors belong in Git only. A ledger block is reserved for academically consequential decisions or events.
