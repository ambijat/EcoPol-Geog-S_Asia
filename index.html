01 · Purpose

From scattered files to teachable knowledge

Historical decks, published PDFs, books, maps, evidence notes, hosted materials, and new analysis belong to one intellectual history. This project makes their identities and relationships visible without silently rewriting the past.

Technical validity is not academic approval. The system prepares evidence and preserves lineage; the instructor decides what is true, useful, and ready to teach.

02 · Architecture

Three working layers, one course

The interfaces have distinct jobs. They do not compete for authority and none replaces academic judgement.

A

Artifact cockpit

A native desktop workspace for repository archaeology, scanning, candidate inspection, registration, provenance, and relationships.

B

Production cockpit

A browser workspace for lecture pairs, evidence metadata, slide planning, validation, class records, and bounded generation.

C

Native production path

A PySide6 migration path for the lecture-production workflow, developed in parallel against the browser cockpit’s acceptance baseline.

03 · Method

A bounded scholarly workflow

The project joins archaeology-first preservation with classroom-backward production: begin with what exists, then work toward the next viable lecture.

  1. DiscoverRead source locations without reorganising or ingesting them.
  2. InspectIdentify candidates, duplicates, formats, and present condition.
  3. RegisterGive governed artifacts stable identity and symbolic location.
  4. RelateRecord publication, derivation, support, and summary relationships.
  5. TeachBuild, review, approve, conduct, and preserve the lecture record.

04 · Artifact ontology

Six classes keep unlike things distinct

A deck, a source note, a map, and a published PDF may support the same lecture, but they are not interchangeable. Classification protects meaning.

03

05

Conceptual structures and portable relationship views.
06

Spatial material

Maps, country and regional evidence, and geographic datasets.

05 · Course production

Fifteen weeks, paired by design

Each weekly unit is developed as a connected Tuesday–Friday teaching pair. Editable decks, classroom PDFs, teaching briefs, source maps, verification records, and post-class records form the complete deliverable.

Tuesday · Part A · 9:00–11:00

Frame and establish

Introduce the historical, spatial, conceptual, and evidentiary architecture of the weekly problem.

Friday · Part B · 9:00–11:00

Extend and interrogate

Deepen the argument through regional comparison, political economy, evidence, and discussion.
