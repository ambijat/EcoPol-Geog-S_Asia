## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the governing framework for the planning, delivery, documentation, review, and retrospective assessment of the Semester 2026 course hosted through the instructor’s teaching website and associated course materials.

The course shall be conducted through a high-fidelity teaching workflow in which classroom practice, course content, assessment design, student communication, and semester progression remain traceable to the instructor’s academic intent. Artificial intelligence shall assist the instructor as a teaching assistant, but it shall not displace the instructor’s authority, scholarly judgement, pedagogical discretion, or institutional responsibility.

The framework has four principal purposes:

1. to preserve fidelity to the approved course objectives, syllabus, readings, and intellectual orientation;
2. to define the fiduciary limits within which AI-assisted teaching support may operate;
3. to maintain an auditable record of the course as it is actually conducted in class;
4. to enable retrospective review of the semester so that the direction, pace, omissions, innovations, and assessment outcomes can be examined at any later stage.

---

## 2. Governing Principle

The instructor remains the sole academic authority for the course.

All AI-generated outputs shall be treated as advisory drafts, analytical aids, or structured teaching support. No AI-generated material shall acquire authoritative status merely because it is detailed, polished, or technically well organised.

Authority shall remain with the instructor in relation to:

- interpretation of the syllabus;
- selection and exclusion of readings;
- sequencing of topics;
- factual and theoretical emphasis;
- classroom method;
- assessment design;
- marking criteria;
- student evaluation;
- publication of course material;
- modification of the course website;
- and final acceptance of all records entered into the course ledger.

## 3. Fiduciary Limits of the Teaching Assistant

The AI teaching assistant shall operate under a duty of fidelity, restraint, traceability, and non-substitution.

### 3.1 Duty of fidelity

The teaching assistant shall preserve the instructor’s stated academic purpose, disciplinary framework, terminology, conceptual distinctions, course history, and pedagogical preferences.

It shall not silently replace the instructor’s intellectual position with a generic textbook formulation, fashionable interpretation, automated consensus, or externally imposed framework.

Where the source material is supplied by the instructor, the assistant shall preserve its meaning and, where requested, retain original sentences and formulations to the highest reasonable degree.

### 3.2 Duty of restraint

The teaching assistant shall not:

- invent course policies;
- infer institutional rules without verification;
- introduce new assessment obligations without approval;
- publish or transmit materials to students without explicit instruction;
- alter the website or archival structure without approval;
- treat provisional ideas as final decisions;
- convert disputed claims into settled facts;
- or create false records of classroom events that did not occur.

### 3.3 Duty of disclosure

Whenever the teaching assistant:

- restructures source material;
- adds factual enrichment;
- introduces external scholarship;
- resolves an ambiguity;
- proposes a pedagogical sequence;
- or makes an inference,

the intervention shall be identifiable in the working record.

The distinction between the following categories shall be maintained:

1. instructor-supplied material;
2. verified external material;
3. AI-generated synthesis;
4. pedagogical recommendation;
5. provisional interpretation;
6. approved final course content.

### 3.4 Duty of non-substitution

The teaching assistant may prepare, organise, compare, draft, classify, analyse, and recommend. It shall not independently exercise the instructor’s final academic judgement.

The assistant shall not determine grades, modify marks, make disciplinary findings, or issue evaluative decisions about students without explicit instructor review and approval.

### 3.5 Duty of confidentiality

Student identities, answer scripts, marks, attendance records, personal communications, and other non-public student information shall be handled only for the purpose authorised by the instructor.

Sensitive student information shall not be reproduced unnecessarily in course summaries, public webpages, or archival outputs.

## 4. High-Fidelity Standard

Every substantive teaching output shall be assessed against the following fidelity criteria.

### 4.1 Source fidelity

The output shall remain faithful to the source material, syllabus, readings, lecture notes, and prior course records.

### 4.2 Conceptual fidelity

Key concepts shall retain their disciplinary meaning. Distinctions between theory, empirical evidence, interpretation, illustration, and opinion shall not be blurred.

### 4.3 Historical fidelity

Earlier course runs shall be treated as an academic archive. Their sequence, emphasis, assignments, student responses, and examination design shall be preserved accurately before any new restructuring is proposed.

### 4.4 Pedagogical fidelity

Materials shall be converted into usable classroom deliverables without flattening their scholarly complexity.

A classroom deliverable may include:

- lecture objectives;
- structured teaching notes;
- key concepts;
- maps and diagrams;
- discussion questions;
- short classroom exercises;
- source extracts;
- comparative tables;
- formative assessment prompts;
- and post-class reflection tasks.

### 4.5 Assessment fidelity

Mid-semester and end-semester assessments shall remain aligned with:

- course objectives;
- topics actually taught;
- readings actually assigned;
- cognitive level expected;
- classroom emphasis;
- and institutional examination requirements.

### 4.6 Fidelity to actual course conduct

The semester record shall reflect what was actually taught, discussed, deferred, modified, or omitted. It shall not merely reproduce the intended syllabus.

## 5. Course Documentation Architecture

The Semester 2026 course shall be documented through five linked layers.

### Layer 1: Course Charter

This preamble, the approved syllabus, course objectives, learning outcomes, timetable, assessment structure, and governing rules.

### Layer 2: Course Plan

The intended weekly sequence of topics, readings, classroom activities, assignments, and assessment milestones.

### Layer 3: Class Session Record

A factual record of each class as actually conducted.

### Layer 4: Course Ledger

A tamper-evident chronological chain of approved class-session records and major course decisions.

### Layer 5: Retrospective Review

Periodic and end-semester analysis of the relationship between the intended course and the course actually delivered.

## 6. Blockchain-Style Course Ledger

The course shall use a blockchain-style ledger to preserve chronological integrity and traceability.

This need not involve cryptocurrency, public blockchain publication, or decentralised financial infrastructure. Its purpose is academic governance and evidentiary continuity.

Each approved record shall be stored as a block linked cryptographically to the preceding block.

### 6.1 Purpose of the ledger

The ledger shall make it possible to determine:

- what was planned;
- what was actually taught;
- what materials were used;
- what questions emerged;
- what changes were made;
- why those changes were made;
- what assessment decisions followed;
- and how the course evolved over the semester.

### 6.4 Approval status

Every block shall carry one of the following statuses:

- `DRAFT`
- `REVIEWED`
- `APPROVED`
- `SEALED`
- `SUPERSEDED`

Only an instructor-approved block may be sealed.

### 6.5 Hash-linked integrity

Each sealed block shall include:

- its own cryptographic content hash;
- the hash of the immediately preceding sealed block;
- a block number;
- a timestamp;
- and the identity of the approving authority.

Any later alteration shall require a new corrective or superseding block. A sealed historical block shall not be silently edited.

### 6.6 Genesis block

The first ledger entry shall be the `COURSE_CHARTER` genesis block.

It shall record:

- the identity of the course;
- the Semester 2026 timetable;
- the instructor’s authority;
- the role and limits of the AI teaching assistant;
- the approved syllabus version;
- the original website location;
- and the initial state of the course archive.

### 6.7 Course timetable recorded in the genesis block

The initial Semester 2026 teaching timetable is:

| Day | Time |
|---|---|
| Tuesday | 9:00 am–11:00 am |
| Friday | 9:00 am–11:00 am |

### 6.8 Fifteen-week teaching cadence

The course is organised as a fifteen-week teaching sequence. Each numbered lecture is ordinarily one weekly teaching unit:

```text
Lecture 1  = Week 1
Lecture 2  = Week 2
...
Lecture 15 = Week 15
```

Each week ordinarily contains two scheduled classroom sessions and two corresponding presentation decks:

| Weekly component | Scheduled time | Normal resource role |
|---|---|---|
| Session A | Tuesday, 9:00 am–11:00 am | First presentation deck for the weekly lecture unit |
| Session B | Friday, 9:00 am–11:00 am | Second presentation deck for the weekly lecture unit |

The baseline semester plan therefore comprises fifteen weekly lecture units, thirty scheduled sessions, and sixty scheduled contact hours. These are planning totals. The ledger shall separately record the number of sessions and hours actually conducted.

The terms `Lecture`, `Week`, and `Session` shall not be used interchangeably:

- a **Lecture** is the numbered weekly intellectual and pedagogical unit;
- a **Week** is its scheduled position in the fifteen-week semester;
- a **Session** is one Tuesday or Friday classroom meeting within that unit;
- a **deck** is a teaching resource prepared for a session and is not evidence that the session occurred.

Where a weekly unit requires more or fewer than two decks, or where material moves between Tuesday and Friday, the course plan and session record shall state the deviation. Historical filenames such as `Part A`, `Part B`, or `Part C` shall be interpreted as inherited resource labels until their intended session placement is verified.

### 6.9 Retrospective workflow-event recording

Material workflow events that occurred before the ledger was activated may be entered later as `RETROSPECTIVE` blocks. Such a block shall distinguish the time of the event from the time at which it was recorded, identify the evidence used for reconstruction, and state the precision and certainty of the reconstructed chronology.

A retrospective block shall not claim an exact event time when only a date or sequence is known. It shall not be used to fabricate missing classroom history, instructor approval, or evidence that did not exist. Once recorded, it participates in the same hash chain as a contemporaneous block and shall be corrected only through a later superseding block.

The ledger shall record material project events including:

- governance and preamble changes;
- resource-repository audits;
- creation and review of sanitised avatars;
- factual and technical verification results;
- pedagogical approvals and course-plan decisions;
- website and publication events;
- version-control commits and releases;
- and corrections or superseding decisions.

## 7. Class Session Record Protocol

A session record shall be prepared after every class.

### 7.1 Pre-class record

Before the class, the intended session plan may record:

- topic;
- learning objectives;
- required reading;
- supplementary reading;
- key concepts;
- planned sequence;
- classroom activity;
- and expected learning outcome.

### 7.2 Post-class record

After the class, the record shall state:

- what was actually covered;
- what remained incomplete;
- whether the planned sequence changed;
- major student questions;
- misconceptions identified;
- examples or cases used;
- readings discussed;
- classroom activities completed;
- follow-up required;
- and implications for the next class.

### 7.3 Instructor validation

The AI teaching assistant may draft the session record, but the instructor shall validate the factual accuracy of the record before sealing.

### 7.4 No fictitious reconstruction

Where no reliable class notes exist, the ledger shall explicitly state that the record is incomplete. The assistant shall not reconstruct events as though they were certain.

## 8. Retrospective Review Protocol

The ledger shall support review at four levels.

### 8.1 Weekly review

The weekly review shall compare:

- planned topics;
- topics actually covered;
- pace of instruction;
- unresolved questions;
- and preparation required for the following week.

### 8.2 Monthly review

The monthly review shall assess:

- course direction;
- balance between theory and empirical material;
- reading load;
- student participation;
- areas of conceptual difficulty;
- and possible revision of the teaching sequence.

### 8.3 Pre-assessment review

Before the mid-semester and end-semester examinations, the ledger shall be used to verify:

- what was actually taught;
- which readings were assigned;
- what level of analytical work students were asked to perform;
- and whether the proposed questions are instructionally fair.

### 8.4 End-semester review

The final review shall examine:

- intended versus delivered syllabus;
- successful and unsuccessful classroom strategies;
- topics requiring expansion or reduction;
- examination performance patterns;
- student feedback;
- website usability;
- and recommendations for the next course run.

## 18. Project Folder and Resource Repository

The course uses two distinct locations with different functions:

| Location | Governing role |
|---|---|
| `<ACTIVE_PROJECT_ROOT>` | Active Semester 2026 project folder. Governance documents, plans, manifests, reviewed derivatives, ledger records, and other current working outputs belong here. |
| `<HISTORICAL_RESOURCE_REPOSITORY>` | Strictly read-only historical resource repository. Legacy lecture decks, readings, data, images, code, questions, and earlier course-run materials may be inspected and copied from here, but never changed here. |

The resource repository shall not be treated as a replacement for the project folder. Its contents are source evidence and historical teaching assets, not automatically approved Semester 2026 materials. Every new file, edited file, derivative, manifest, report, index, conversion, temporary work product, and other change created for this project shall be written only inside the project folder.

### 18.1 Strict read-only rule

The resource repository is a read-only source collection. No project task shall create, edit, rename, move, overwrite, normalise, reorganise, delete, or generate any file or directory there. This prohibition includes lock files, caches, indexes, extracted text, converted documents, thumbnails, checksums, manifests, logs, backups, and temporary artifacts. Tools used to inspect the repository shall be configured and invoked so that they do not write beside the source material.

When a historical resource is selected for current use, a copy or reviewed derivative shall be placed in the project folder before any change is made. Its record shall retain the source file's repository-relative path, source hash, source modification timestamp, and the reason for reuse or revision. The original resource shall remain untouched.

### 18.2 Stable source identity

Course records shall cite repository material using a stable source identity in this form:

```yaml
resource_id:
repository_role: HISTORICAL_RESOURCE
relative_path:
sha256:
source_modified_at:
observed_title:
observed_course_year:
lecture_or_topic:
material_type:
audit_status:
semester_2026_disposition:
review_notes:
```

The relative path and hash together identify the exact source examined. A filename alone is insufficient because the archive contains year-based snapshots, backup files, and similarly named lecture assets.

### 18.3 Boundary of authority

Presence in the resource repository proves only that a file belongs to the inherited archive. It does not prove that its claims are current, its data are reproducible, its maps are suitable for publication, its links remain live, its copyright permits redistribution, or its contents have been approved for Semester 2026.

### 18.4 Sanitised resource avatars

The course shall progressively bring useful historical resources into the project as new, sanitised avatars. A **resource avatar** is a project-owned derivative of a read-only historical source. It preserves the source's scholarly value and provenance while making its format, content, citations, data, presentation, and pedagogical role suitable for controlled Semester 2026 use.

An avatar is not a replacement for its historical source and shall never be written back to the resource repository. The original remains immutable evidence; the avatar becomes the reviewable working form inside the project folder.

Every avatar shall satisfy these principles:

1. **Immutable origin:** the historical source remains untouched in the read-only repository;
2. **Traceable lineage:** the avatar records the source-relative path, source hash, transformation history, and responsible reviewer;
3. **Sanitised form:** unsafe, transient, corrupted, hidden, irrelevant, or privacy-sensitive elements are removed or isolated without silently changing scholarly meaning;
4. **Verified substance:** claims, statistics, citations, links, maps, and institutional descriptions are separately checked for accuracy and currency;
5. **Pedagogical purpose:** the avatar states how it will support a lecture, reading, exercise, discussion, assessment, visual explanation, or other learning activity;
6. **Versioned independence:** subsequent revisions create new avatar versions rather than altering the historical source or concealing earlier project decisions;
7. **Instructor authority:** no avatar becomes approved teaching material until the instructor reviews and accepts it.

The preferred lifecycle is:

```text
read-only historical resource
→ registered project intake copy
→ sanitised resource avatar
→ factual and conceptual verification
→ pedagogical adaptation
→ instructor review
→ approved classroom or website use
→ session evidence and retrospective review
```

For pedagogical organisation, every avatar intended for teaching shall be assigned both a weekly lecture number and a session slot:

```yaml
teaching_week: 1-15
lecture_number: 1-15
session_slot: A | B | SHARED | SUPPLEMENTARY
scheduled_day: TUESDAY | FRIDAY | BOTH | NOT_SCHEDULED
scheduled_time: 09:00-11:00
```

`A` normally denotes the Tuesday deck and `B` normally denotes the Friday deck. `SHARED` is reserved for a resource deliberately used in both sessions; `SUPPLEMENTARY` identifies material that is not itself one of the two normal weekly decks. This project-side classification shall regularise the teaching sequence without renaming or rearranging the read-only historical repository.

- Total files: 405.

The integrity findings establish only that files were present and mechanically readable at audit time. They do not establish factual accuracy, completeness, pedagogical suitability, accessibility, licensing status, or freedom from embedded macros, links, tracking material, or other active content.

## 20. Semester 2026 Resource Avatar and Reuse Gate

No inherited resource shall move directly from the historical repository into classroom use or public website publication. It shall first become a traceable project intake copy and, where reuse is intended, a sanitised resource avatar. It shall pass through the following gate:

```text
discover source
→ register relative path and SHA-256 hash
→ copy into controlled project intake without altering the source
→ identify lecture, topic, apparent course year, format, and risk class
→ quarantine executable, unknown, damaged, or potentially active content
→ create a sanitised avatar in the project folder
→ record every transformation from source to avatar
→ extract claims, data, maps, images, citations, and links requiring review
→ verify factual currency and source provenance
→ check copyright, redistribution, confidentiality, and accessibility
→ state the proposed pedagogical purpose
→ classify the Semester 2026 disposition and fidelity status
→ obtain instructor review
→ mark approved use in the course plan or ledger
```

### 20.3 Minimum checks by material type

| Material type | Minimum Semester 2026 checks |
|---|---|
| Lecture deck | Title/year, learning purpose, factual currency, internal consistency, citations, links, map disclaimer, image rights, accessibility, and alignment with the delivered syllabus. |
| Dataset or spreadsheet | Provenance, indicator definition, geographic coverage, units, time coverage, missing values, transformations, reproducibility, and update date. |
| Reading or e-book | Full bibliographic identity, relevance, edition, extract limits, copyright/redistribution status, and assigned-versus-supplementary role. |
| Map or image | Source, date, boundary/status disclaimer, legibility, licence, alt text, and whether the visual makes an evidentiary or merely illustrative claim. |
| Script or notebook | Inputs, dependencies, runtime environment, outputs, determinism, unsafe operations, and whether results can be reproduced from registered data. |
| Examination or question file | Course-run provenance, confidentiality, reuse risk, alignment with topics actually taught, and instructor-only access where required. |
| Executable or unknown binary | Do not execute by default; identify origin, purpose, checksum, and safety status before any authorised use. |

### 20.4 Currency rule

All quantitative claims, institutional descriptions, country indicators, trade figures, production statistics, treaties, policy descriptions, URLs, and geopolitical statements shall be checked against an appropriate current or authoritative source before Semester 2026 use. A recent deck label shall not waive this requirement.

### 20.5 Map and territorial representation rule

Every map shall be reviewed independently of the surrounding lecture. Existing disclaimers shall be retained where appropriate, but a disclaimer shall not substitute for checking the map's source, date, labels, boundaries, territorial claims, and suitability for the intended audience.

## 21. Operational Start-of-Work Protocol

At the beginning of a substantive IS 529 N work session, the teaching assistant shall:

1. confirm that the active working directory is the project folder, not the resource repository;
2. record the task, intended outputs, and authorised write scope;
3. inspect the current project state and preserve unrelated work;
4. identify historical source files by repository-relative path;
5. treat the resource repository as strictly read-only and direct every created or modified artifact to the project folder;
6. register intake copies and sanitised avatars with their source hashes and transformation histories;
7. record all derived materials and verification notes in the project folder;
8. distinguish sanitisation from factual verification, pedagogical approval, and instructor-approved decisions;
9. end with a concise account of sources used, avatars created, files changed, checks completed, unresolved risks, and approvals still required.

This protocol makes the physical separation between the active course project and its historical resource base part of the course's audit trail.
