### C1 · Opening enquiry and scope

- **Central concept:** South Asia is the lecture’s object of enquiry, but its
  extent and regional meaning require examination.
- **Teaching purpose:** establish lecture identity, scope, initial map and the
  question that the inherited opening currently leaves implicit.
- **Internal sequence:** title and course identity → indicative political map →
  boundary disclaimer → analytical question.
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** the coloured South Asia map on slide
  2 and the statement that maps are indicative.
- **Repetition or drift:** little repetition; the defect is under-framing. A map
  appears before criteria for defining the region.
- **Verification requirements:** map source, date, territorial coverage,
  boundary representation and the adequacy of the disclaimer.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `REVISE`. Preserve identity but make the enquiry
  explicit in a future authorised derivative.
- **Through-line:** opens “what South Asia is” while signalling that maps are
  one way of knowing, not a self-sufficient definition of region.

### C2 · Indigenous spatial imaginaries and ways of knowing

- **Central concept:** South Asia can be known through Puranic cosmology,
  sacred geography, dialogue, pilgrimage, rivers and symbolic ecology.
- **Teaching purpose:** show that regional knowledge predates modern
  state-cartography and is produced through culturally situated practices.
- **Internal sequence:** ontology (3) → epistemology and dialogue (4) →
  pilgrimage and source frame (5) → anthroposphere/dvīpa explanation (6) →
  visual and textual evidence (7–12).
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** Valdiya; Puranic texts; Jambudvīpa,
  Bharatavarsha, Mount Meru, pilgrimage routes, river narratives, dvīpa tables
  and maps.
- **Repetition or drift:** slides 3–6 repeat ontology, epistemology and dvīpa
  material at exceptional density; slides 7–12 alternate useful evidence with
  long source reproductions. Cosmology, geology and modern regional location
  sometimes become indistinguishable.
- **Verification requirements:** every Valdiya attribution; Meru–Pamir
  identification; claims that symbolic narratives encode tectonics or climate;
  intentional location of shrines; lists and descriptions of dvīpas; textual
  quotations; and any mapping of Puranic categories onto modern geography.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `MERGE`. Retain the distinctive intellectual purpose
  but consolidate repetition only in a future teacher-authorised derivative.
- **Through-line:** contributes both “what South Asia is” as a cultural-spatial
  formation and “how it is known” through non-Cartesian sources and practices.

### C3 · Historical accounts, observers and source positions

- **Central concept:** different observers and source forms construct different
  regional pictures through rivers, routes, cities, science, empire and trade.
- **Teaching purpose:** compare sources rather than present all accounts as a
  single cumulative description of South Asia.
- **Internal sequence:** Greek/classical frame (13–16) → medieval and Arab
  accounts (17–18) → Megasthenes and Al-Biruni (19–20) → British and historical
  geography sources (21–23) → coins and inscriptions (25).
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** Strabo, Ptolemy, Eratosthenes,
  classical river accounts, Arab geographers, Megasthenes/Arrian, Al-Biruni,
  the Imperial Gazetteer, ancient-geography works, coins and inscriptions.
- **Repetition or drift:** slide 19 returns to Megasthenes after the medieval
  heading; biographical summaries dominate source comparison; slide 24
  interrupts the source sequence and belongs in C4.
- **Verification requirements:** original-versus-mediated source status;
  chronology; quotations and translations; Porus/Chandragupta comparison;
  claims about Arab observations and Indian science; “religious federalism”
  and “national consciousness”; Al-Biruni’s travels with Mahmud; bibliographic
  identity and relevance of each displayed book or extract.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `REVISE`. Reorganise by source position and evidentiary
  limits rather than by accumulated description.
- **Through-line:** principally answers “how South Asia is known” and shows that
  regional knowledge changes with observer, medium and purpose.

### C4 · Boundaries, maps and territorial frames

- **Central concept:** environmental features, cartographic choices, empires
  and colonial rule produce overlapping but non-identical regional frames.
- **Teaching purpose:** distinguish physical, historical and political
  boundary-making instead of using maps as automatic proof of regional unity.
- **Internal sequence:** environmental definition and alternative extents (24)
  → mapping as an imperial practice (26) → religious-geographical map (42) →
  Mauryan frame (43) → colonial frame (44).
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** boundaries named on slide 24;
  “Mapping an Empire”; South Asian religion map; Mauryan and British imperial
  maps. River and city material on slides 14–16 and mapping on slide 23 are
  supporting cross-references.
- **Repetition or drift:** physical barriers, environmental connections,
  historical reach and modern membership are compressed together on slide 24;
  slides 42–44 present maps with little explanation of scale, date or purpose.
- **Verification requirements:** map provenance, date, legend and territorial
  assumptions; the scope of Afghanistan, Iran, Tibet and neighbouring regions;
  SAARC membership/observer statements; Ghaggar-Hakra claim; historical limits
  of the Mauryan and colonial maps; and what each map can legitimately show.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `REVISE`.
- **Through-line:** demonstrates why no single map settles “what South Asia is”
  and makes region the organising concept connecting physical, historical and
  political frames.

### C5 · Peoples, culture, stratification and agrarian society

- **Central concept:** mobility, religion, language, caste and agrarian
  relations make South Asia internally diverse yet historically connected.
- **Teaching purpose:** establish the human and social content of regional
  formation without reducing the region to civilisational uniformity.
- **Internal sequence:** section frame (27) → migration/invasion and agrarian
  history (28) → religion/philosophy (29) → language diversity (30) → caste and
  social stratification (31) → land, agriculture and caste (32).
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** historical artwork, religious images
  and books, Ethnologue charts, caste diagram/interview and a Kerala caste
  article.
- **Repetition or drift:** slides operate as a broad survey rather than a
  regional argument; agriculture appears on slides 28 and 32; caste is split
  across 31–32 without a clear transition from language or religion.
- **Verification requirements:** source dates and licences; language categories
  and counts; caste/class terminology; applicability of an Indo-Pakistani
  generalisation; the Kerala case’s representativeness; and claims connecting
  agriculture, caste and empire.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `MERGE`.
- **Through-line:** supplies “what South Asia is” as a differentiated human
  landscape and tests whether diversity weakens or constitutes regionality.

### C6 · Uneven development and population

- **Central concept:** population scale and development inequalities are
  relational features of the region, not isolated country facts.
- **Teaching purpose:** compare patterns of population, poverty and economic
  change while avoiding obsolete statistics and spectacle.
- **Internal sequence:** poverty (33) → economy (34) → regional population map
  (37) → cartogram (38) → historical anthroposcape (39) → Uttar Pradesh/global
  comparison (40).
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** poverty head-count chart, GDP per
  capita charts, WMO-linked population map, cartogram, historical map and the
  Uttar Pradesh population comparison.
- **Repetition or drift:** population is revisited in C10; slides 37–40 shift
  from regional pattern to visual scale comparisons without a single date or
  question; poverty/economy charts are disconnected from social differentiation.
- **Verification requirements:** date, unit, source and country coverage for
  every chart; poverty thresholds; constant/current currency; population year;
  comparability of cartograms; and the current validity and pedagogical purpose
  of the Uttar Pradesh comparison.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `REVISE`.
- **Through-line:** tests what kind of region South Asia is through asymmetry
  and uneven development, while making evidence quality central to how it is
  known.

### C7 · Region as an analytical concept

- **Central concept:** a region combines spatial relations, scale, territory,
  social interaction, organisation, identity and possible institutional
  capacity; these dimensions need not advance together.
- **Teaching purpose:** give one economical diagnostic framework before
  applying it to South Asia.
- **Internal sequence:** theory section (45) → Schmitt-Egner/Hettne sequence
  (46) → scale, action space and action unit (47) → environment/organisation
  relation (48) → globalisation and re-regionalisation (49) → geographical and
  political concepts (50–51).
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** Schmitt-Egner, Hettne, Keating,
  Blotevogel, Weichart, Giddens, Strabo and Sauer; hard/soft region examples.
- **Repetition or drift:** definitions repeat across 46–48 and 50–51; “territory
  as actor”, “action unit” and “action space” are insufficiently distinguished;
  slide 49 broadens abruptly to globalisation. Speaker notes on slides 45–51
  contain the unresolved placeholder `<number>`.
- **Verification requirements:** exact quotations and page references;
  attribution and ordering of Hettne’s degrees; the non-inevitable nature of
  regionness; terminology around territory and actorness; Strabo/Sauer claims;
  hard/soft region definitions; and the dated reference to OAU.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `MERGE`.
- **Through-line:** makes region explicit as the organising concept and provides
  criteria for asking both what South Asia is and how claims about it are made.

### C8 · Testing South Asia as a region

- **Central concept:** South Asia can be tested through networks, geographical
  delimiters, civil society, security, economic interaction and institutions.
- **Teaching purpose:** move from general region theory to a bounded South Asia
  application.
- **Internal sequence:** candidate dimensions (52) → network, ethnoscape and
  geography definitions (53) → regional objectives and constraints (54).
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** civil-society convergence,
  India–Pakistan security, entrepôt and capital-flow claims, SAFTA, visa-free
  movement, transport and trust deficit.
- **Repetition or drift:** slides list dimensions rather than test them;
  geographical contiguity and functional connection are treated as conclusions;
  institutions are named without evidence of performance.
- **Verification requirements:** current SAARC/SAFTA operation; trade, transit
  and mobility evidence; accuracy of visa claims; capital flows; security
  framing; and whether civil-society convergence is supported.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `REVISE`.
- **Through-line:** directly asks in what senses South Asia is a region and
  exposes the difference between regional connection and collective capacity.

### C9 · Political formation, identity and geopolitics

- **Central concept:** state formation, conflict, democracy, identity,
  India-centred asymmetry, great-power involvement and nationalism make South
  Asia a politically contested regional formation.
- **Teaching purpose:** explain why material connection does not produce
  political unity and distinguish internal regional dynamics from external
  geopolitics.
- **Internal sequence:** conflict and democracy cases (35–36) → modern
  state/nation framing (55) → governance challenges (56) → Indian
  heartland/core claims (57–58) → great powers (59) → world-order perspectives
  (60) → Johnson synthesis (61).
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** 2018 conflict map, election images
  and links, state/nation assertions, Indian identity and foreign-policy
  categories, imperial/Cold War/China–US claims, and Johnson’s democracy,
  pluralism and nationalism frame.
- **Repetition or drift:** slides 55–61 repeatedly restart with “such a South
  Asia”; the cluster expands into a preview of the entire course; India-centred
  claims risk substituting one national frame for regional analysis; internal
  politics and great-power geopolitics are not separated.
- **Verification requirements:** all conflict allegations and dates; democracy
  comparisons; state-before-nation claims; “Indian heartland” and geocultural
  core claims; labels such as anti-fundamentalism; Cold War/post-9/11/post-2014
  periodisation; China–US spheres; largest Muslim-population and “strongest
  credentials of democracy” claims; and the Johnson edition/page.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `MERGE`.
- **Through-line:** explains why region remains the useful scale for analysing
  connection, asymmetry and contestation without presuming political unity or
  actorness.

### C10 · Comparative indicator appendix

- **Central concept:** country shares and fragility indicators can illustrate
  regional asymmetry only when their definitions, dates and sources are clear.
- **Teaching purpose:** provide optional comparative evidence after the
  argument, not substitute pie charts for a conclusion.
- **Internal sequence:** land share (62) → population share (63) → economy
  share (64) → Fragile States Index (65).
- **Maps, evidence, examples and claims:** three country-share charts and the
  2023 Fragile States Index table.
- **Repetition or drift:** duplicates population/economy material from C6;
  follows the closest inherited synthesis at slide 61 and therefore weakens the
  close; slide 63 visibly duplicates country labels and values.
- **Verification requirements:** source year, country set, denominator and
  rounding for all shares; the duplicated Bangladesh and Afghanistan labels on
  slide 63; GDP measure on slide 64; FSI methodology, year and classroom
  relevance on slide 65.
- **Preliminary ruling:** `MOVE_TO_NOTES` until a current, sourced comparative
  set is commissioned and the instructor decides whether it belongs in class.
- **Through-line:** potentially demonstrates uneven regional structure, but in
  its inherited form it does not securely establish what South Asia is or how
  it should be known.

   problem of mediated accounts is largely absent.
3. **Religion, language, caste and agrarian relations** (27–32, with 42): these
   form a substantial inherited human-geography survey but have no dedicated
   synopsis heading.
4. **Conflict and electoral democracy as distinct regional problems** (35–36):
   absorbed into “divided politics” without retaining their specific teaching
   questions.
5. **Population imaginaries and cartographic scale** (37–40): reduced to a
   generic population comparison.
6. **Globalisation and re-regionalisation** (49): not carried explicitly into
   the synopsis.
7. **Hard/soft regions and region as action space/action unit** (47, 51):
   condensed into the capacities framework.
8. **India as geocultural core and contested regional identity** (57–58):
   largely omitted, despite occupying two inherited slides.
9. **Great-power and world-order geopolitics** (59–60): absent as a distinct

10. **Johnson’s democracy, pluralism and nationalism frame** (61): not

These omissions are not automatically faults in the synopsis. They identify
material that requires an instructor decision before any derivative is planned.

## 4. Synopsis concepts weakly grounded in the inherited deck

1. **The explicit space–connections–power route** is a useful synopsis design,
   but the inherited deck never states or follows that route.
2. **Modern cross-border interdependence in water, coasts and energy** is only
   partially supported. Rivers appear mainly in historical accounts; energy is
   not substantively developed.
3. **Partition as the change in regional frame** is not explicitly taught in
   the inherited deck, although colonialism, borders and India–Pakistan rivalry
   are present.
4. **Relational human development across poverty, health and education** is
   weak. The master contains poverty, economy and population graphics but
   little health or education evidence.
5. **Institutional ambition versus effectiveness** rests mainly on brief SAARC,
   SAFTA, movement and trust-deficit bullets.
6. **An ordered judgement of uneven coherence** is a synopsis synthesis, not an
   inherited evidentiary result.
7. **Evidence discipline** is a valid governance layer but not inherited
   lecture content.
8. **The working conclusion** has no direct inherited counterpart; slide 61 is
   only a partial source-based summary.
9. **The bridge to Friday/Himalayas** can be inferred from mountain and river
   material but is not present as a teaching transition.
